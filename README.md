# SmokingHealth-Analysis
## 1. Executive Summary 
### The dashboard provides an overview of smoking status, blood-pressure risk categories, patient demographics and selected health-condition indicators. The headline cards show approximately 2K total patients, a current-smoker metric displayed as 0.29, and 1,050 recorded conditions. The smoking-status and blood-pressure-risk charts indicate that never-smokers form the largest smoking-status group, while Elevated is the largest BP risk group in the displayed data. This report is a visual interpretation of the screenshot only. It is not a clinical assessment and should not be used to infer causation between smoking and health outcomes. 
## 2. Key Dashboard Indicators
### The key dashboard indicators show an overall patient count rounded to approximately 2,000 (≈ 2K), with a current smoker metric of 0.29 that likely represents 29% if stored as a proportion, though its formatting should be confirmed before publishing. Additionally, the summary card displays 1,050 recorded conditions, which requires clarification on whether the figure reflects unique patients or total condition records. Regarding specific breakdowns, never-smokers form the largest smoking status group at 764 compared to 508 current and 502 former smokers, while an elevated reading is the largest blood-pressure risk category at 873, followed by 605 in the lower range and 296 in the high-risk category.
## 3. Smoking-Status Analysis 
### The smoking-status doughnut chart displays approximately 764 never-smokers (43.0%), 508 current smokers (28.6%) and 502 former smokers (28.3%). Current and former smokers together account for just over half of the displayed smoking-status distribution. This is descriptive only and does not establish that smoking caused any condition shown elsewhere. 
## 4. Blood-Pressure Risk Category
### The BP Risk Category chart shows 873 patients (49.2%) in Elevated, 605 (34.1%) in Lower Range and 296 (16.7%) in High. Elevated is the largest category in the displayed breakdown. The screenshot does not define category thresholds; document these before clinical or operational interpretation. 
## 5. Demographic and Regional Views 
### The dashboard includes patient counts by region and smoking status, patient counts by age, current smokers by age group, and patient counts by gender. The regional chart compares North, West, South, East and Central. Male and Female are the two largest gender groups, while Other is much smaller. Exact values are not legible enough to report reliably. The current-smokers-by-age-group visual shows a prominent bar for the 18–29 group. Since slicer selections may affect the visual and the screenshot does not make all filter states clear, validate this in the interactive report. 
## 6. Health Conditions and Blood-Pressure Measures 
### A chart compares health-condition counts across smoking-status groups, but individual condition labels and values are too small to read accurately. Another visual compares average systolic and diastolic blood pressure by smoking status. Systolic bars appear around the low 110s to approximately 120, and the diastolic line around the mid-70s; these are visual estimates, not verified measurements. 
## 7. Key Observations 
### • Never-smokers are the largest smoking-status segment; current and former smokers are similar in size. 
### • Elevated is the largest BP risk category, representing about 49.2% of that chart. 
### • The dashboard combines demographic, geographic, smoking and health-condition views. 
### • Some chart labels, exact values and filter states cannot be confirmed from a static screenshot. 
## 8. Recommendations for Dashboard Improvement 
### • Format Current Smoker % as 29% if the underlying measure is 0.29; verify the measure first. 
### • Define the thresholds for Elevated, Lower Range and High BP risk categories. 
### • Add data labels or tooltips to regional, age, gender and condition charts. 
### • Display the reporting period, refresh date and record count used by each visual. 
### • Clarify whether Recorded Conditions counts condition records, diagnoses or unique patients. 
### • Make active slicer selections obvious and consider a reset-filters control. 
### • Avoid causal language unless supported by an appropriate study design.
